The method

I don't always have time for an idea when I have it. This is how I start it anyway.

  1. Brainstorm. Draw the idea out of yourself, with AI helping you spitball.
  2. Blueprint. A loop of targeted prompts, each with its own objective, turns the idea into a blueprint full of data and findings. The last one looks across everything for emergent findings.
  3. Starter repo. A stronger model turns the blueprint into a starter repo, and more rounds develop it, ending with another look for emergent opportunities. What comes out is a strong starter repo snapshot.

Every result goes into a folder I call Ready for Work. When time allows, it's easy to jump in: the project is already warmed up.

1 The brainstorm

There's no fixed prompt here, and that's the point: the brainstorm is about you, not the model. You're drawing an idea out of yourself, and the AI is there to help you spitball.

This step can run on less expensive models. A more expensive one is worth it for a deeper brainstorm.

Watch for: on cheaper models, be hyper-critical of sycophancy, and of a model agreeing with an idea that can't work.

2 The blueprint loop

Once you have the idea, run these in order, in one conversation. Put your idea where the first one says (OBJECTIVE).

Watch for: context has to be managed. It's easy to spend too many turns on the blueprint, to the point where more development before the final emergent pass does harm.

3 The starter repo

Next, a stronger model turns the final blueprint into a starter repo. Then these rounds develop it, in order.

Watch for: the repo zip and attached files can grow pretty big, and some plans and services handle that better than others.

Ready for Work

That's the chain. What it makes waits in Ready for Work: every repo in that folder came from prompting a model with a blueprint that stemmed from an idea. When I finally have time for one, it's warmed up, then kept on the burner.
